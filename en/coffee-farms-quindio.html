How to Get to Coffee Farms by Car

From Armenia (El Edén Airport)

30–50 minutes depending on the farm. Take the Armenia–Salento road for farms near Salento, or head toward Quimbaya for El Jazmín. Pick up your car at El Edén Airport.

From Pereira (Matecaña Airport)

1 hour to 1 hour 30 minutes via the Autopista del Café through Armenia, then onward to Salento or Quimbaya. Pick up at Matecaña Airport.

From Salento

5–20 minutes to the nearest farms (El Ocaso, Don Elías, Las Acacias). Short, scenic drives through coffee plantations. Rent from Salento.

From Quimbaya

About 10 minutes to Finca El Jazmín. Quimbaya is also the gateway to the National Coffee Park and PANACA.

Best Coffee Farms to Visit in Quindío

Finca El Ocaso (Salento)

The most recommended coffee farm tour in the region. El Ocaso offers a complete bean-to-cup experience that includes walking through the coffee fields on a beautiful hillside, learning how cherries are picked by hand, watching the washing and drying process, and finishing with a roasting demonstration and cupping session. The views from the farm across the green valley are stunning, and the bilingual guides make the experience accessible to English-speaking visitors. El Ocaso is about 15 minutes from Salento town by car, on a mostly paved road with a short gravel section at the end.

  • Full bean-to-cup tour with roasting
  • Bilingual guides available
  • Book in advance recommended

Finca Don Elías (Salento)

A family-run farm offering an intimate, artisanal coffee experience. Don Elías is smaller than El Ocaso, which means groups are kept small and the tour feels personal. The family has been growing coffee for generations and shares their knowledge with genuine warmth. You will walk through the plantation, learn about organic growing methods, and taste freshly brewed coffee on the farm porch overlooking the valley. The road from Salento is partly unpaved — a crossover vehicle provides a more comfortable ride, though compact cars can make it in dry conditions.

  • Family-run, intimate groups
  • Artisanal organic methods
  • Crossover vehicle recommended

Las Acacias (Salento)

A well-established classic farm tour that has welcomed visitors for years. Las Acacias is known for its thorough explanation of the entire coffee production cycle, from seedbed nurseries to export-ready beans. The farm is surrounded by shade trees (acacias, as the name suggests) that create a beautiful canopy over the coffee plants. The tour includes a tasting of different roast levels so you can appreciate how roasting transforms the flavor profile. Located just outside Salento on an accessible road.

  • Well-established, comprehensive tour
  • Shade-grown coffee under acacias
  • Accessible road from Salento

Finca El Jazmín (Quimbaya)

Set in the municipality of Quimbaya rather than Salento, El Jazmín offers a less crowded alternative with traditional Quindío architecture — a classic bahareque farmhouse surrounded by coffee plants, banana trees, and tropical flowers. The tour covers the full coffee process and includes a walk through the farm grounds where you can also see cacao and plantain cultivation. Because fewer tourists know about El Jazmín, it provides a more relaxed, authentic atmosphere. Just 10 minutes from Quimbaya town on a paved road.

  • Traditional bahareque architecture
  • Less crowded than Salento farms
  • Easy paved road from Quimbaya

Practical Information for Farm Visits

Book in Advance

Most farms require or strongly recommend advance bookings, especially during high season. Contact farms via WhatsApp or their websites to reserve your spot.

Best Time of Day

Morning tours are ideal. The light is best for photos, temperatures are comfortable, and you have time to visit a second farm or explore Salento in the afternoon.

Harvest Seasons

Main harvest (cosecha): October–January. Second harvest (mitaca): April–June. During harvest you see the full process in action. Farms are open year-round.

What to Wear

Wear comfortable closed-toe shoes suitable for walking on uneven terrain. Bring sunscreen and a hat. A light rain jacket is wise since mountain showers can appear quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to book coffee farm tours in advance?

Yes, we recommend booking in advance, especially during high season (December–January and June–August). Most farms accept bookings via WhatsApp or their websites. Walk-ins are sometimes possible but not guaranteed.

Do I need a 4x4 to reach the coffee farms?

Most farms near Salento are accessible by paved or well-maintained gravel roads. A compact car like the Kia Picanto works for the main farms. For more remote farms with rural tracks, a crossover like the Kia Seltos or Mazda CX-30 provides extra comfort.

When is coffee harvest season?

The main harvest (cosecha principal) runs from October to January. A smaller harvest called mitaca happens from April to June. Farms are open year-round, but during harvest you can see the full picking and processing in action.

Do you accept international driving licenses?

Yes. We accept international driving licenses from any country, including all EU nations. We also accept payments in USD and offer bilingual service.

How long does a coffee farm tour take?

Most tours last 1.5 to 2.5 hours, covering the full bean-to-cup process: picking, washing, drying, roasting, and tasting. Some farms offer extended experiences with lunch or additional activities.

Can I visit multiple farms in one day?

Yes. With your own rental car you can visit 2–3 farms in a day, especially the ones near Salento that are close to each other. Having a car gives you flexibility that organized tours cannot match.

How to Get to Los Nevados by Car

From Manizales

65 km to the park entrance via the Manizales–Murillo road. The first section is paved; the final stretch is unpaved and requires 4x4. Estimated time: 1 hour 30 minutes. Pick up at La Nubia Airport or Manizales city.

From Pereira

120 km via Pereira–Manizales and then to the park entrance. Estimated time: 2 hours 30 minutes. Pick up at Matecaña Airport.

From Armenia

250 km via Armenia–Pereira–Manizales and onward to the park. Estimated time: 3 hours 30 minutes. We recommend spending a night in Manizales before heading to Los Nevados.

What to See & Do in Los Nevados National Park

The Volcanoes

Los Nevados protects several volcanic peaks in the Central Andes. Nevado del Ruiz (5,321 m) is the most famous, an active volcano whose snow-capped summit dominates the skyline. Nevado del Tolima (5,215 m) is a near-perfect cone popular with experienced trekkers. Nevado de Santa Isabel (4,965 m) has lost most of its glaciers in recent decades but remains a striking peak. Together they form one of the most dramatic volcanic landscapes in South America.

  • Park entrance fees apply — check with Parques Nacionales
  • 4x4 required for access roads

Páramo Ecosystem

The páramo is a unique high-altitude grassland ecosystem found only in the tropical Andes. Los Nevados contains some of the most pristine páramo in Colombia, carpeted with frailejones (Espeletia plants) — distinctive rosette-shaped plants that can take decades to grow. Walking through the páramo feels otherworldly: a vast open landscape of golden grasses, silver-leafed frailejones, and crystal-clear streams, all above the cloud line at 3,500 to 4,500 meters.

High-Altitude Trekking

The park offers trekking routes ranging from half-day walks near the entrance to multi-day expeditions. Popular routes include the trek to Laguna del Otún (a high-altitude lake at 3,950 m) and the demanding multi-day ascent of Nevado del Tolima. Trails pass through páramo grasslands, past volcanic hot springs, and above the cloud forest zone. The landscape changes dramatically with every hundred meters of altitude gained.

Wildlife & Birdwatching

Los Nevados is home to Andean condors, mountain tapirs, spectacled bears, and a rich variety of hummingbirds. The transition zones between cloud forest and páramo are especially productive for birdwatching. Early mornings offer the best chances to spot condors riding the thermals above the volcanic ridgeline. The park's remoteness and altitude keep visitor numbers low, making wildlife encounters more likely.

Practical Information

4x4 Required

The access roads to Los Nevados are unpaved, steep, and can be muddy after rain. A 4x4 vehicle is strongly recommended. Do not attempt these roads in a standard car.

Extreme Cold

Temperatures range from 0–10°C (32–50°F) and can drop below freezing at night. Bring thermal layers, a waterproof jacket, gloves, and a warm hat. UV protection is essential at altitude.

Altitude Sickness

The park ranges from 3,500 to 5,300 meters. Altitude sickness is a real possibility. Spend at least one day acclimatizing in Manizales (2,150 m) before ascending. Hydrate well and ascend gradually.

Fuel & Permits

Fill up your tank in Manizales — there are no gas stations on the way to the park. Entry permits may be required; check with Parques Nacionales Naturales de Colombia for current regulations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a 4x4 for Los Nevados National Park?

Yes. A 4x4 vehicle is strongly recommended. The roads leading to the park entrance are unpaved, steep, and can be muddy. We recommend our Toyota Fortuner or Toyota Hilux.

How long does it take to hike in Los Nevados?

It depends on the trail. Half-day hikes are available near the park entrance. Multi-day treks to Nevado del Tolima or Santa Isabel take 2 to 4 days. Check with the park for current trail availability.

Is Los Nevados safe?

Yes, with proper preparation. The main risks are altitude sickness (the park ranges from 3,500 to 5,300 meters) and rapidly changing weather. Acclimatize in Manizales first and bring warm, layered clothing.

Do I need a guide?

Some areas of the park require a certified guide. Check with Parques Nacionales Naturales de Colombia for current regulations. For basic trails near the entrance, a guide may not be mandatory but is recommended.

When is the best time to visit?

The driest months are December to February and June to August. Clear weather gives the best views and safest hiking conditions. Avoid the wettest months (April, May, October, November) if possible.

What should I bring?

Warm layers (temperatures drop to 0°C), waterproof jacket, sunblock (UV is intense at altitude), water, snacks, and sturdy hiking boots. Fuel up your car in Manizales before departing.
